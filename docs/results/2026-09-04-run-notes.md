# Run notes — 2026-09-04

**Commands**

```bash
python3 run.py --ticks 40000 --seed {1,2,3} --attack --width 64 --height 64 \
  --out runs/carn-s{1,2,3}.csv
python3 run.py --ticks 40000 --seed 1 --width 64 --height 64 --out runs/herb-s1.csv
python3 run.py --ticks 40000 --seed 1 --attack --no-mutation --width 64 --height 64 \
  --out runs/nomut-s1.csv
```

Grid 64×64 rather than the spec's 128×128, and 40,000 ticks rather than 50,000,
so that three seeds plus controls fit in available time. All tuning in this
project was done at 64×64, so the runs are comparable to each other.

## Headline

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 1. Brain complexity grew | **Supported**, 3/3 seeds — and predation is the cause (5-9x vs herbivore control) |
| 2. Diet gene separated into modes | **Not supported** — the population collapsed to herbivory in 3/3 seeds |
| 3. A behaviour appeared that was never coded | **Supported** — convergent body plans, killing as interference competition, predator-prey cycles |
| 4. New variation beats founder sorting | **Supported on body genes** (speed 1.96 vs 1.07); **not supported on habitat choice** |

The single most useful run was the `--no-mutation` control. Without it, "body
genes moved" looks like evolution when much of it is selection culling the
founding pool. Without the `--attack`-off control, "small and fast" looks like a
predator-escape adaptation when it is purely metabolic. Both readings would have
been wrong and both would have been publishable-looking.

## Claim 1 — brain complexity grew

| | seed 1 | seed 2 | seed 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| mean links, final | 12.86 | 16.94 | 15.71 |
| mean links, peak | 15.61 | 17.06 | 15.98 |
| hidden nodes, final | 3.01 | 2.62 | 1.87 |
| hidden nodes, peak | 5.53 | 2.62 | 2.22 |
| generations | 93 | 105 | 87 |

All start at exactly 10.00 links and 0 hidden nodes. Growth in 3/3 seeds.

**Important qualification.** Mutation adds connections *by construction*, so
growth alone shows the machinery turns, not that the structure is useful. The
`--no-mutation` control is trivially flat (structure can only change by
mutation), so it cannot distinguish "useful" from "merely present". The stronger
evidence for selection is the body-gene convergence below, not this table.

Complexity is not a ratchet. In seed 1 hidden nodes rose to 5.53 by tick 21,000
and fell back to 3.01 by 40,000, tracking the population crash — structure is
rented every tick and gets shed when the ecosystem can no longer pay for it.

## Claim 2 — diet separated into modes: NOT SUPPORTED

Final mean diet: 0.114, 0.026, 0.094 across the three seeds. All three collapsed
toward pure herbivory. Sarle's bimodality coefficient reached 0.949 (seed 1),
well above the 0.555 threshold, **and that statistic is misleading here.** The
actual histogram at tick 40,000:

```
  0.0-0.1   76 ############################################################
  0.1-0.2   33 ##########################
  0.2-0.3   44 ##################################
  0.3-1.0    0   (empty)
```

The whole population lies below diet 0.3. The two clusters are sub-structure
*within* herbivory, not the herbivore/carnivore split the claim was about.
Sarle's coefficient is inflated by skew against the hard floor at 0. Reporting
the coefficient without plotting the histogram would have produced a false claim
of speciation.

**Why carnivory lost.** This is a predicted consequence of a deliberate design
choice (spec §7): a killer gains no energy directly, and the corpse is a public
good anyone can eat. Carnivory therefore requires two separately-evolved
behaviours — kill, then feed — and pays nothing for the first alone. The design
note at the time said this "makes carnivory meaningfully harder to bootstrap,
which is a feature if you want it to be a real evolutionary achievement, and a
problem if you want to see predators by generation 200." The second reading is
what happened.

## Claim 3 — a behaviour appeared that was never coded: SUPPORTED

### Predation drives brain complexity (strongest result)

Same seed, same constants, one flag different (`--attack`):

| | herbivore control | carnivory s1 | s2 | s3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mean links, final | **10.19** | 12.86 | 16.94 | 15.71 |
| hidden nodes, final | **0.34** | 3.01 | 2.62 | 1.87 |
| generations | 80 | 93 | 105 | 87 |
| population, final | 140 | 153 | 165 | 110 |

Five to nine times more hidden neurons when agents can kill each other.
Generations (80 vs 87-105) and populations (140 vs 110-165) are comparable, so
this is not a turnover or sample-size artefact. A world of plants alone does not
reward cognition; a world that contains things which kill you does.

This is the cleanest causal claim in the project because exactly one variable
changed and the control shares the seed.

### Convergent body-plan evolution — metabolic, NOT anti-predator

| | seed 1 | seed 2 | seed 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `size` start → final | 1.75 → 0.58 | 1.80 → 0.54 | 1.68 → 0.53 |
| `speed` start → final | 1.29 → 1.98 | 1.19 → 1.95 | 1.19 → 1.95 |

Three independent lineages from different random founders converged on the same
body plan: minimum size, maximum speed. The statistical signature is the
between-seed spread, which **fell** from 0.053 to 0.021 for `size` and from 0.046
to 0.014 for `speed`. Under drift, between-run variance grows. It more than
halved while both genes marched to opposite ends of their ranges.

Both genes are pinned at their bounds (`size` range 0.5–3.0, `speed` range
0.5–2.0), so the ranges are now a binding constraint — evolution is pushing
harder than the genome permits, and the true optimum lies outside the box.

**The herbivore control rules out predation as the cause.** With `--attack` off,
the same seed reaches `size` 0.52 and `speed` 1.98 — statistically
indistinguishable from the carnivory runs' 0.55 and 1.96. Without this control
the obvious reading would have been "small and fast evolved to escape
predators", and it would have been wrong.

The driver is metabolic: `repro_threshold` scales with `size`, so small agents
reach reproduction sooner, and `upkeep` scales with `size²`. Nothing in the code
expresses "be small and fast"; it falls out of the energy accounting.

### Killing as interference competition, not predation

Seed 1 recorded 6,987 kills — 53% of all deaths — while mean diet fell to 0.114.
Agents kill constantly and do not eat the corpses. The behaviour is not trophic:
removing a neighbour frees plants and space, and that indirect benefit sustains
the killing without any nutritional payoff. Ecologists call this interference
competition. It was not designed in; the attack output and the energy economy
were, and this fell out of them.

### Predator–prey style oscillation

Population is smooth to ~22,000 ticks, then breaks into sustained
large-amplitude cycles (roughly 40–270 agents) as the plant layer is stripped and
regrows. Visible in `runs/carn-s1.png`, top-left panel.

## New variation DOES outperform founder sorting (on body genes)

The `--no-mutation` control shares seed 1 with the carnivory runs, so the only
difference is whether reproduction introduces variation:

| | no-mutation (sorting only) | carnivory mean (mutation on) |
|---|---|---|
| mean links | 10.00 → **10.00** | → 15.17 |
| hidden nodes | 0.00 → **0.00** | → 2.50 |
| `size` | 1.75 → **1.16** | → 0.55 |
| `speed` | 1.29 → **1.07** | → 1.96 |
| `diet` | 0.483 → **0.557** (up) | → 0.078 (down) |
| kills | **57** | 5,116 |

Founder sorting moves `size` partway, from 1.75 to 1.16 — it can only pick the
best genome that already exists in the founding pool. Mutation reaches 0.55, far
beyond anything a founder carried. On `speed` sorting achieves nothing at all
(1.29 → 1.07, slightly *down*) while mutation pins it at the ceiling. `diet`
moves in opposite directions under the two regimes.

**This corrects the earlier negative verdict.** On the rich/poor habitat world,
mutation did not beat sorting (0.674 vs 0.727) — but habitat occupancy is a poor
metric, because consumption equalises the halves (ideal free distribution) and
sorting alone can reach a good distribution. Measured on body-gene optimisation
instead, new variation clearly outperforms sorting. The original gate asked the
wrong question rather than getting a null answer.

## Not established

- **Evolvability evolving.** Seed 1's mutation rate fell to 0.075 during the
  stable phase and rose to 0.106 during the oscillating phase, exactly as spec
  §5.1 predicts. Seeds 2 and 3 ended at 0.084 and 0.142 with no comparable
  pattern. One seed out of three is an anecdote, not a result.
- **New variation outperforming founder sorting.** Measured separately on the
  rich/poor habitat world over ~230 generations: mutation on 0.674 vs control
  0.727, Cohen's d = −0.71 *in the control's favour*. Recorded as an `xfail` in
  `tests/test_validation.py`.

## Bugs this run surfaced

1. **Recurrent brain state diverged silently.** Node state reached 1.096e308 by
   tick 16,055 with a max weight of 3.5, because `identity`/`relu` are unbounded
   and cycles are permitted. Invisible from outside — outputs are `tanh` and stay
   in range — until state overflows to `inf`, sums go `nan`, and `argmax` returns
   arbitrary actions. **Every result from a run longer than ~16,000 ticks before
   this fix was suspect**, which is why the carnivory runs here were re-run from
   scratch after the fix. Guarded with `STATE_LIMIT = 1e6`.
2. **The plant layer was not a cellular automaton.** Growth was
   `PLANT_GROWTH * (1 + PLANT_SPREAD * n)`, giving every cell the full rate
   regardless of neighbours. Fixed to be neighbour-driven.
3. **`plant_spontaneous = 0.01` was over-tuned for patchiness** and caused
   extinction on both seeds by 15,000 ticks. 0.05 survives 60,000 and is *more*
   patchy.
