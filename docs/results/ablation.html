The Scrambled Brain

Predation drives brain complexity in this world — but mutation adds structure by construction, so bigger brains only prove the machinery turns. The falsifying question is whether that structure earns its keep. Destroy it and see who copes.

What was carried over

One population, and the wrong ruler

The first pass found a single population — predation seed 1, carrying 5.28 hidden nodes — where scrambling the evolved weights produced a large, internally replicable effect. It verified the manipulation was bit‑for‑bit inert where there was nothing to scramble. Two problems remained, and closing both is what this pass did.

It rested on that one population. And it was measured by feeding rate, which is one component of fitness rather than fitness itself — awkwardly, scrambled brains fed more.

The lesion Weights are permuted among connections touching a hidden node, in the genome, so descendants inherit the scrambling. Connectivity, weight magnitudes, node count and brain rent are preserved exactly. Only the learned mapping dies. Both arms fork from one evolved population with a deep copy, sharing an identical world and RNG state.

Step one · screen wider

Sixty fresh seeds found nothing like it

If seed 1 were a normal draw that three seeds happened to sample, screening harder would produce more like it. Sixty new seeds were evolved for 20,000 ticks each with predation on, sharded across nine cores.

Seeds 1, 2 and 3 were re‑run as a control and reproduce their recorded values exactly (5.275, 1.09, 1.764), so this is genuine seed variation and not a regression. Screening does not solve the n = 1 problem. It converts it into a measured fact: substantial structure evolves in roughly one population in sixty.

Step two · fix the ruler

The old window was measuring its own noise

The better readout is cohort fitness: tag the agents alive at lesion time, then ask how many of those specific individuals survive and how many offspring they leave. The denominator is fixed the moment the lesion lands, so it cannot be inflated by survivorship.

It was first run over a 1,500‑tick window. That turns out to be far too long.

The result

Seed 1 separates completely. Nothing else moves.

Every population below was scrambled ten independent times and measured over 500 ticks. The question for each is simple: does the intact value fall outside the entire range of its ten scrambled twins?

All six arms. “Outside” asks whether intact falls beyond every one of the ten scrambled replicates.

Predation seed 1 is unambiguous on both measures. Intact survival of 0.4335 sits against a scrambled range of 0.060–0.185; intact offspring of 0.605 against 1.880–2.545. Not one of ten independent scrambles came near it in either direction.

Underneath, nothing. Populations at 2.71, 1.76 and 1.09 hidden nodes are flat — there is no gradient, just a gap and then seed 1. And peaceful seed 3, carrying 0.10 hidden nodes, returns values identical to the last digit across all ten scrambles. With nothing to scramble the manipulation changes nothing, bit for bit. The apparatus stays validated.

The direction

The structure is doing work. The work is restraint.

The two halves of cohort fitness point opposite ways. Scrambled agents survive about four times worse — and leave about 3.8 times more offspring. At the end of the window the scrambled populations are larger: 211 intact against a scrambled range of 267–389.

Over 500 ticks, destroying the evolved brain helps. The structure implements a slow life history: feed less, breed less, live longer.

This is the same direction the discarded feeding result pointed, now confirmed on reproduction too. Which raises the obvious suspicion: that scrambled agents are simply spending down a reserve, and a longer horizon would present the bill.

The horizon

No bill arrives

Seed 1, run 10,000 ticks past the lesion, intact against five independently scrambled twins.

Unplanned observation Over those 10,000 ticks the intact population sheds structure, 5.28 → 3.03 hidden nodes, while the scrambled arms hold or grow theirs (4.54–6.66). Selection is trimming the very structure this experiment set out to defend. Not interpreted here — it is one seed and could be drift.

Verdict

Three claims, three confidence levels

Solid

The apparatus is exact. The lesion is bit‑for‑bit inert when it has no target — identical output across ten replicates on a 0.10‑hidden‑node population, on both measures. Whatever the other arms show, it is not an artefact of the manipulation.

Solid within one population

The structure is not decorative. In seed 1, scrambling the evolved weights changes behaviour enormously — four times worse individual survival, 3.8 times more offspring, every one of ten replicates outside intact on both measures. This structure is doing real work.

Not established

That work is not shown to be worth anything. Over 500 ticks the scrambled populations end larger; over 10,000 ticks none go extinct and their mean populations run slightly higher. The structure buys individual longevity and pays for it in fecundity, and the trade does not come out ahead on any population measure tested. It also remains a single population — sixty fresh seeds produced nothing above 2.71 hidden nodes, so it cannot currently be replicated at all.

Next

Stop comparing separate worlds

Every comparison here pits one deterministic intact trajectory against a handful of scrambled ones in separate worlds, then asks whether the numbers overlap. That is the weakest form of the question.

The decisive version is direct competition in a single world: scramble half the population in place, tag both groups, and let the lineages compete for the same food under the same predators. Relative fitness is then read off which lineage's descendants take over, with no cross‑world comparison at all.

That needs a heritable lineage tag on Agent propagated through _reproduce — about three lines of instrumentation that does not touch the dynamics. Deliberately not added yet: it is a new experiment, not a step in the one that was planned.
