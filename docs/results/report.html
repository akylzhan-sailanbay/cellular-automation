What evolved, what didn't,
and what the controls killed

An agent-based world with neural genomes, asexual reproduction, and no fitness function anywhere in the code. Three seeds, two controls, 40,000 ticks each.

The finding that matters most

The two control runs changed the conclusions more than the three experimental runs did. Without the mutation-off control, “body genes moved” reads as evolution when much of it is selection culling the founding pool. Without the predation-off control, “small and fast” reads as a predator-escape adaptation when it is purely metabolic. Both wrong readings would have looked entirely publishable.

Predation drives brain complexity

Same seed, same constants, one flag different. This is the cleanest causal claim in the project, because the control shares its random seed with the experiment and generations and population sizes are comparable — so it is not a turnover or sample-size artefact.

Three independent lineages from different random founders reached the same body plan: minimum size, maximum speed. The load-bearing statistic is the between-seed spread, which fell from 0.053 to 0.021 for size and from 0.046 to 0.014 for speed. Under drift, between-run variance grows. It more than halved while both genes marched to opposite ends of their ranges.

The predation-off control (column four) lands in the same place, which rules out the intuitive reading. This is not an escape adaptation. repro_threshold scales with size and upkeep with size², so small agents simply breed sooner. Nothing in the code says “be small and fast”; it falls out of the energy accounting.

Both genes are pinned at their limits, so the gene ranges are now a binding constraint — the true optimum lies outside the box I gave evolution to work in.

New variation beats founder sorting — on the right metric

The mutation-off column above is the interesting one. Selection with no new variation can only pick the best genome that already exists: it moves size from 1.75 to 1.16 and achieves nothing at all on speed (1.29 → 1.07, slightly down). With mutation, lineages reach 0.55 and 1.96 — values no founder carried.

This corrects an earlier negative result. Measured on habitat occupancy, mutation did not beat sorting: 0.674 against 0.727, Cohen's d of −0.71 in the control's favour. But habitat occupancy is a poor instrument here, because consumption equalises the rich and poor halves — an ideal free distribution arising on its own — so sorting alone already reaches a good distribution. The original test asked the wrong question rather than getting a null answer.

The statistic said speciation. The histogram said no.

Sarle's bimodality coefficient peaked at 0.949, far above the 0.555 threshold. Plotting the actual distribution of the diet gene at tick 40,000:

0.1–0.233
0.2–0.344
0.3–1.00

Diet 0 = pure herbivore, 1 = pure carnivore. The entire population sits below 0.3.

There are no carnivores. The two clusters are sub-structure within herbivory, and the coefficient is inflated by skew against the hard floor at zero. Reporting the statistic without plotting the distribution would have produced a confident, false claim of speciation.

Carnivory losing is a predicted consequence of a deliberate design choice: a killer gains no energy directly, and the corpse is a public good. Predation therefore needs two separately-evolved behaviours — kill, then feed — and pays nothing for the first alone.

Killing without eating

Seed 1 recorded 6,987 kills, 53% of all deaths, while mean diet fell to 0.114. Agents kill constantly and do not eat the corpses. The behaviour is not trophic: removing a neighbour frees plants and space, and that indirect benefit sustains the killing with no nutritional payoff. Ecologists call this interference competition. It was not designed in.

Full run, seed 1

Not established

  • Evolvability evolving. Seed 1's mutation rate fell to 0.075 during the stable phase and rose to 0.106 during the oscillating phase, exactly as designed-for. Seeds 2 and 3 ended at 0.084 and 0.142 with no comparable pattern. One seed of three is an anecdote.
  • Adaptive value of the extra brain structure. Mutation adds connections by construction, so growth alone shows the machinery turns, not that the structure earns its keep. The body-gene comparison carries that claim; the link counts do not.

Bugs the runs surfaced

  • Recurrent brain state diverged silently. Node state reached 1.1×10³⁰⁸ by tick 16,055 with a maximum weight of 3.5, because identity and relu are unbounded and cycles are permitted. Invisible from outside — outputs are tanh and stay in range — until state overflows to infinity, sums become nan, and the action selector returns arbitrary moves. Every result from a run longer than ~16,000 ticks was suspect, so all runs here were repeated after the fix.
  • The plant layer was not a cellular automaton. Growth gave every cell the full rate regardless of neighbours, so an isolated cell filled to capacity in 133 ticks and the equilibrium was a uniform lawn — precisely the condition that removes any selection gradient for navigation.
  • Over-tuning for patchiness caused extinction. Making stripped regions recover 100× slower produced beautiful patches and killed both seeds by 15,000 ticks. The value that survives 60,000 ticks is also the more patchy one.

Simulation core contains no fitness function, no hand-coded predator or prey roles, and no unseeded randomness; all three are enforced by tests. Same seed produces byte-identical output. Reproduce with python3 run.py --ticks 40000 --seed 1 --attack --width 64 --height 64.
